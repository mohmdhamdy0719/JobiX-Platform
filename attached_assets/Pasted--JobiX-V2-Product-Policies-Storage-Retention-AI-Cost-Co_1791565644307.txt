## 1. Export Rate Limits

Our proposed product policy per user is:

- At most 1 new logical PDF export every 5 minutes.
- At most 3 new logical exports per rolling hour.
- At most 5 new logical exports per rolling 24 hours.
- At most 10 new logical exports per rolling 30 days.
- At most one active export intent for the same user/profile at a time.

Requirements:
- Enforce limits authoritatively on the server/database, not only in Flutter or the Edge Function.
- Limits must be race-safe under concurrent requests and across multiple service instances.
- Retries and replay of an already accepted idempotency key must not count as a new export or create another debit.
- Export Again is a new logical export and counts toward limits.
- Count failed and refunded logical attempts toward abuse/cost limits where appropriate, without charging the user for a failed export contrary to the existing Credit contract.
- Use clearly defined rolling windows and UTC/server time, not client clocks.
- Return a useful retry-after/error response and show it in the app.
- Ensure legitimate existing PDF downloads remain free and do not count as new exports.
- Explain how these limits interact with the existing five-jobs-per-10-minute rule. Recommend one consistent policy rather than overlapping/conflicting controls.

Do not implement the proposed numbers until the existing policy, relevant SQL functions, idempotency rules, and side effects have been mapped.

## 2. Insufficient Credits and Preview Admission

Proposed behavior: if the user does not have enough available Credits for a task, do not start that paid task. Return a clear insufficient-Credits error.

First inspect the actual behavior and cost of:
- Resume preview/canonical document preparation.
- New PDF export.
- Export Again.
- Downloading an existing valid PDF.
- Any planned AI generation.

For each action, report whether it currently costs Credits, incurs meaningful infrastructure cost, or both.

Important:
- Do not silently convert a currently free preview or download into a paid feature.
- Do not block access to a previously generated, still-valid PDF merely because the user's current Credit balance is low, unless the existing approved contract explicitly requires it.
- For paid operations, check available Credits and admission limits before expensive work wherever possible.
- Use authoritative reservation and finalization logic; never trust a client-supplied balance.
- Where an operation is intentionally free, protect it with separate request, byte, and compute limits rather than pretending it consumes Credits.
- Propose clear Arabic/English UI error messages and retry behavior.

Identify exactly which operations should be blocked by insufficient Credits, and request approval if the proposed behavior would change existing product semantics.

## 3. First-Signup Bonus: Five Credits Once

Our intended business rule:
- Grant 5 signup Credits only once to an eligible first-time user.
- The account must be confirmed/verified before the grant is issued.
- Signing out and registering a second account must not grant another 5 Credits if the same person has already claimed the first-time bonus.
- A second confirmed account must receive 0 signup bonus Credits.
- The client must never be able to request or forge a grant.

Inspect the current signup trigger, Credit ledger, Auth providers, confirmation flow, unique constraints, and existing account records. Propose a secure, idempotent implementation and a migration/backfill strategy for existing accounts.

Explicitly explain the limitations of identifying the same human across multiple accounts, app reinstalls, cleared local storage, or multiple devices. Do not claim that device identifiers, IP addresses, email confirmation, or Google sign-in alone can guarantee one bonus per human. Avoid invasive fingerprinting or collecting unnecessary personal data.

Recommend a privacy-conscious combination of verified identity, server-side grant records, abuse signals, and a global signup-grant budget. Explain how account recovery, deleted accounts, duplicate Auth events, concurrent signups, and provider linking should behave.

Preserve the immutable Credit ledger and existing successful-export charging rules. Do not remove or rewrite historical ledger entries.

## 4. PDF Retention: Seven Days and Hard Delete

The intended user-facing policy is:
- A successfully generated PDF can be downloaded for seven days.
- After seven days, the PDF is no longer downloadable through any app/API route.
- The underlying PDF object should be physically deleted as far as the storage provider and approved retention policy permit, to control storage costs.
- Expired or missing PDFs must not silently regenerate or charge Credits.
- Export Again creates a new export from the current profile under the existing Credit rules.

Inspect:
- Logical expiration and download authorization.
- GCS lifecycle rules, object generations, versioning, soft delete, retention locks, backups, and any other applicable retention mechanism.
- Whether signed download URLs can remain usable past the logical expiry.
- The exact behavior of deletion, stale workers, in-flight uploads, and cleanup failures.
- Database export jobs, immutable snapshots, source data, logs, and backup retention separately from PDF objects.

Important:
- Seven-day PDF retention does NOT automatically authorize deleting immutable snapshots or other records needed by the existing contract.
- Do not disable soft delete or shorten any retention configuration without a documented policy review and explicit approval.
- Explain the difference between immediate application-level unavailability, asynchronous lifecycle deletion, and physical deletion of recoverable object generations.
- Determine whether existing signed URLs could outlive the promised availability period. Propose a safe fix if needed.
- Provide an explicit object/database deletion matrix showing what is deleted, when, by which mechanism, and what can remain in backups or recovery windows.

Do not claim immediate physical deletion is guaranteed unless the effective provider configuration supports it.

## 5. HTML Preview and Snapshot Storage

The production-readiness report observed an average stored HTML snapshot size of approximately 0.99 MB in a very small sample. We do not want HTML previews or immutable export snapshots accumulating indefinitely without a defined retention/storage budget.

Inspect the real preview implementation and distinguish:
1. Temporary preview HTML that can be regenerated.
2. Canonical immutable HTML snapshots tied to an accepted export.
3. Profile/resume source data.
4. Generated PDF objects.
5. Database backups, logs, and other retained artifacts.

Propose a storage policy that:
- Avoids permanently storing disposable previews.
- Reuses cached preparation only when source versions, template versions, ownership, and authorization are safe.
- Preserves immutable export correctness and the ability to verify existing exports.
- Defines an approved retention period or storage budget for each data category.
- Includes cleanup jobs, idempotency, deletion-race protection, monitoring, and safe failure behavior.
- Does not delete data needed by an active export, a valid download, or the approved deletion/recovery contract.
- Considers compression, deduplicating immutable template/font assets, and reducing repeated large HTML responses.

First determine which HTML is actually stored and for how long. Do not propose deleting every snapshot after seven days merely because the PDF expires; identify the product and audit implications and request approval for any policy change.

## 9. Whole-App Cost Controls and Financial Safety

Design layered cost controls for:
- New PDF exports and retries.
- Free preview/preparation.
- Existing PDF downloads and signed URL issuance.
- Signup bonuses and future rewarded Credits.
- AI generations.
- Database growth and egress.
- Invalid requests, repeated failures, and multi-account farming.

Propose race-safe global budgets, per-user quotas, queue admission limits, and a safe degradation mode. Count resource-consuming attempts even when the user's Credit reservation is released. Do not combine operational dollar budgets with the user's Credit ledger.

Recommend provider budget alerts and any supported hard spend caps, but explain which services are affected and how a hard cap could interrupt downloads or recovery. Prefer application-level admission control as the normal mechanism, with provider limits as a carefully planned emergency backstop.
