# JobiX V2 — Production Readiness & Cost-Efficient Launch Plan

Your task is to inspect the existing repository, current architecture, previous assessment reports, and P0 technical decision report, then produce a practical, phased implementation plan.

**Planning and inspection only. Do not implement anything yet.**

## 1. Product and current architecture

JobiX is a career-support application intended to help people improve their career prospects. It is not limited to job search; it should support users throughout their career and job-seeking journey.

The existing stack includes:

- Flutter application with Riverpod and Drift.
- Supabase authentication, PostgreSQL, and Edge Functions.
- Google Cloud Run PDF Export Gateway and private PDF Worker.
- Private Google Cloud Storage for generated PDFs.
- Credit reservations and an immutable Credit ledger.
- Persistent export jobs, idempotency, worker leases/fencing, upload reconciliation, and deletion safeguards.
- Arabic and English support, including Arabic RTL PDF output.

Inspect the actual repository and existing reports to verify these components. Do not assume every component is fully implemented or correctly deployed just because it exists in source code.

The existing architecture separates the Gateway from the PDF Worker. Preserve this separation unless measured evidence supports a change.

## 3. Export quota and failure policy

The intended user-level export policy is:

- 1 new logical export per rolling 5 minutes.
- 3 per rolling hour.
- 5 per rolling 24 hours.
- 10 per rolling 7 days.
- At most 1 active export intent per user/profile.

These are approved product-policy targets, not proof that enforcement is already implemented.

Replace the existing 5-jobs-per-10-minute check with the approved windows rather than stacking additional overlapping user limits.

Required semantics:

- Replaying the same accepted idempotency key must not create a second job or debit.
- An accepted export that fails must release its pending Credit reservation.
- Failed accepted exports count toward user quotas by default.
- Valid existing PDF downloads remain free, even with insufficient Credits.
- Export Again creates a new logical export and counts against quotas.
- Insufficient Credits must block new paid export admission before expensive work where safely supported.
- Preview remains free under the existing product policy unless an owner-approved decision explicitly changes it.
- Never rewrite historical Credit ledger entries.

### Automated quota exception

The owner selected an automatic exception only when objective, trusted, testable evidence proves an internal JobiX failure.

Existing error codes alone are not sufficient evidence.

Plan a narrowly scoped, auditable mechanism that:

- Binds evidence to the exact logical export, worker attempt/fence, and stage.
- Rejects client-supplied failure assertions and ambiguous outcomes.
- Grants at most one exception per eligible logical export.
- Does not modify historical job states or rewrite Credit transactions.
- Does not bypass active-intent, deletion, idempotency, or Credit-accounting safeguards.
- Does not reopen terminal `FAILED` jobs.

The current architecture supports retrying eligible nonterminal failures with the same job and reservation. Terminal `FAILED` jobs are not currently reopened.

Evaluate the proposed behavior: after a verified exception, the user explicitly requests a new export intent with a new idempotency key. The old failed job remains auditable; the new export is charged under existing successful-export rules.

One exception per failed operation must not become an unlimited retry loophole. Assess repeated replacement failures and global infrastructure-attempt budgets without inventing stricter user limits.

Do not finalize an exception allowlist until the actual failure evidence and state transitions have been inspected.

## 4. Reliability and high-concurrency PDF generation

Develop a realistic strategy to handle increasing numbers of users and simultaneous PDF requests without unnecessary infrastructure spending.

Inspect the current Gateway, Worker, database-backed queue, worker leases, fencing, retry logic, upload-flight records, and cleanup/recovery behavior.

Evaluate these areas:

1. **Durable queue and admission control**
   - Ensure accepted jobs survive process restarts.
   - Bound active rendering and queued work.
   - Avoid accepting work beyond safe operational capacity.
   - Measure queue depth and oldest-job age.
   - Ensure user-level quotas and global operational budgets are separate.

2. **Cloud Run scaling**
   - Verify actual deployed min/max instances, CPU, memory, concurrency, timeouts, and billing mode.
   - Determine safe worker concurrency from measured CPU, memory, rendering time, and failure behavior.
   - Evaluate scale-up and scale-down behavior.
   - Avoid increasing maximum instances or keeping instances warm without evidence that the benefit justifies the cost.
   - Check whether higher worker concurrency could overwhelm Supabase or another dependency.

3. **Database protection**
   - Measure connection usage, query latency, lock contention, job-polling load, and queue throughput.
   - Verify atomic admission, per-account locking, reservation recovery, and idempotency under concurrent requests.
   - Avoid excessive polling and unnecessary database writes.

4. **Retry and recovery**
   - Use bounded retries and backoff only where appropriate.
   - Distinguish permanent validation failures from transient internal failures.
   - Handle uncertain outcomes without duplicate jobs, uploads, or debits.
   - Preserve fencing, deletion, and cleanup safeguards.
   - Never retry paid work automatically without an explicitly authorized and tested policy.

5. **Circuit breaker and temporary service protection**
   - Evaluate a service-level circuit breaker for repeated, verified internal failures.
   - Define objective opening, cooldown, recovery, and half-open probe conditions.
   - Distinguish service-wide failures from individual invalid requests.
   - Return a clear temporary-unavailability message and retry-after guidance.
   - Do not let three unrelated user input errors disable the entire service.
   - Assess the owner's proposed temporary cooldown after three consecutive failures, but do not implement it or select arbitrary thresholds without examining the failure classification and blast radius.

6. **PDF rendering efficiency**
   - Measure Chromium startup, font loading, Arabic RTL rendering, HTML size, image processing, CPU, memory, and render duration.
   - Identify safe opportunities to reuse resources, optimize templates, or reduce repeated preparation.
   - Preserve visual consistency, selectable PDF text, A4 formatting, private user data, and preview/export parity.
   - Do not introduce cross-user caching of private resume content.

7. **Preview resource usage**
   - Keep Preview free under the current product policy.
   - Measure preview requests, compute time, response bytes, and database impact separately from paid exports.
   - Investigate whether the preview path can be made lighter without compromising output consistency or usability.

## 5. Cost control and budget safety

The prior cost assessment used Iowa reference prices and an assumed $25.14 monthly baseline. These are sensitivity examples, not verified Doha/Frankfurt bills.

Do not present them as actual costs.

Separate:
- Fixed platform and database costs.
- Cloud Run CPU, memory, requests, and billed instance time.
- Storage, soft-delete recovery, and database growth.
- Network egress and user downloads.
- Failed attempts and retries.
- Preview compute.
- Future AI inference costs.

Identify the exact billing exports, regional SKUs, quotas, usage data, and provider settings required to calculate realistic costs.

Design a configurable, server-enforced global budget strategy that is independent of user Credits.

For any budget that is explicitly approved:
- 50% utilization: operator notification and forecast.
- 80%: investigate usage and projected exhaustion.
- 90%: urgent alert and admission-capacity review.
- Hard ceiling: reject new work in the exhausted cost category safely, with a clear response and preserved accounting/recovery.

These percentages are monitoring-policy targets, not permission to invent arbitrary global quota numbers.

Do not activate previously proposed global limits such as 100 exports/day, 1,500 exports/30 days, 150 render attempts/day, or proposed preparation limits without measured justification and explicit owner approval.

Do not create paid test workloads or increase resource allocations during planning.

Produce a method for measuring actual cost per 1,000 successful exports and preview requests.

## 6. Storage and retention

The current user-facing target is seven-day PDF download availability.

Preserve the distinction between:
- Logical PDF expiry.
- Signed URL expiration.
- Asynchronous GCS lifecycle deletion.
- Soft-delete/version recovery.
- Database snapshot retention.
- Backups and logs.

Do not claim permanent deletion on day seven without evidence.

The previous report's approximately 0.99 MB mean stored HTML was derived from only three snapshots and is not a reliable growth forecast.

Propose a minimal read-only measurement of database growth per export, including relevant tables, indexes, TOAST, and provider-level disk/backup usage, without reading personal resume content.

Do not delete snapshots, shorten soft-delete retention, change versioning/locks, or activate cleanup policies without explicit approval.
